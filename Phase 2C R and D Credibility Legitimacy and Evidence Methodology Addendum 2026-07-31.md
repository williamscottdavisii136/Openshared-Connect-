# Phase 2C R&D Credibility, Legitimacy, and Evidence Methodology Addendum

Date: 2026-07-31
Repository: Openshared-Connect-
Status: Public-safe connector/handoff record

## Purpose

This record preserves the Phase 2C credibility and legitimacy framework for consumer-protection R&D concerning AI agents, AI chatbots, coding agents, terminal agents, model routing, paid hosted routes, open-source routes, and billing transparency.

## Credibility basis

The R&D is strengthened by:

- public-source verification;
- product-specific evidence;
- mode / model / module enumeration;
- default-state capture;
- billing-unit capture;
- lower-cost lawful route comparison;
- open-source / self-host control groups;
- major-platform comparison;
- agency-ready documentation.

## Legitimacy basis

The R&D is framed as public-interest consumer-protection research and billing-transparency documentation. It is designed to determine whether a paid AI product, application, agent, chatbot, coding assistant, terminal assistant, connector, plugin, or hosted route creates excessive effective-cost risk, non-consensual paid-route risk, unclear billing risk, or task-finality risk.

## Open-source distinction

Open-source and self-hosted systems are separately classified as freedom / self-host / user-control and cost-avoidance candidates. Paid hosted wrappers, paid APIs, paid cloud routes, paid model endpoints, paid marketplaces, and paid connectors around open-source code remain subject to billing review.

## Evidence rule

Provider-specific conclusions require provider-specific evidence: pricing pages, terms, screenshots, model picker state, default settings, mode/module selection, token/credit/usage logs, billing statements, proxy receipts, and task-performance comparisons.

## Boundary

This record does not claim official agency, regulator, Better Business Bureau, or public-authority authorization unless separately documented. It does not authorize billing circumvention, subscription circumvention, token/quota/rate-limit evasion, credential capture, session capture, certificate forgery, unauthorized third-party access, public interception, or public service exposure.
